The term "shepherd" is used throughout the Bible in both its noun and verb forms, and can refer to literal shepherding of sheep or as a metaphor of God's unique relationship to His people. This second reference is specifically what is in view of 1 Peter 5:1-4. Through a short survey of key verses spanning from Genesis all the way to Revelation, we will see that Peter's exhortation to the elders of the early church is rooted directly in the example of how God Himself shepherds his people.

God as Our Good Shepherd

To understand what it means for God to be our good shepherd, we turn to Psalm 23 and Isaiah 40:11 . In Psalm 23, David tells us that God leads us in peace and righteousness, protects us, and provides for us all for God's namesake. Similarly, Isaiah explains God's shepherding activity as protection, gathering, carrying, and gently leading His flock. If these are the descriptions that God's Word provides for how He shepherds His people, we can take these characteristics as the model for what a good shepherd should be for God's people.

God Works Through Representatives

While there are examples of God directly shepherding His people in Scripture, more commonly, God works through representatives to lead His people. It is explicitly stated in the Psalms that God "led [his] people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron." Moses also directly appeals to God to appoint a man over the community "so that the LORD's community won't be like sheep without a shepherd."

The Failure of Israel's Shepherds

Even a cursory skim through the Old Testament makes it clear that sinful humanity could never perfectly emulate the qualities of shepherding that God desires for His people. This is called out in Ezekiel 34 . The prophet is told to prophesy against the shepherds of Israel because they have greatly distorted God's intended purposes. These false shepherds have been serving themselves instead of the flock and neglecting their duties. Because of the failures of her leaders, Israel became prey for her enemies, ultimately leading to the Babylonian exile.

Jesus: The Good Shepherd

The penultimate example of the good shepherd that God desires is shown through the incarnate Jesus Christ. In that same chapter of Ezekiel where God calls out failed leaders, God also proclaims that He Himself will shepherd His flock with justice. With this backdrop, Jesus enters the picture. Throughout His ministry He exemplified all the qualities of the good shepherd by healing the sick, feeding thousands, and seeking the lost.

Perhaps the most explicit treatment is in John, where Jesus directly states that He is the good shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep, unlike hired hands who abandon the flock. If we want to understand the right ways to shepherd God's people, there can be no better example than Jesus.

Jesus Raises Up Shepherds

Continuing the same model seen through Moses and Joshua in the Old Testament, Jesus established leaders to help guide His people in the new covenant era. Jesus saw the crowds as sheep without a shepherd and then sent out the disciples to go to the lost sheep of Israel, healing the sick and freely giving rather than serving for gain.

Peter's Exhortation to Church Elders

It is in this context that we turn to 1 Peter 5:1-4. Peter roots the exhortation in the sufferings of Christ and the coming glory of His kingdom. He compels proper motivations for leadership and warns against selfish incentives, greed, and domineering authority. Finally, he calls elders to be examples to the flock.

This call is all-encompassing when we remember that Jesus, the Chief Shepherd, is our example and the standard we should strive for in all shepherding ministry.

Conclusion

In summary, if we wish to truly shepherd God's flock as He intends, we must look to the examples God Himself has provided, always striving to be more like Jesus. God is the only true Good Shepherd for His people, but He desires to use us in His purposes; to that end, we must strive to be faithful servants and good shepherds ourselves.
